# Everly should float glass, ground content

In 2025–2026, Apple, Google and usability researchers have landed on a restrained style. Navigation and controls go on translucent chrome that floats. Content sits on calm, opaque surfaces. Spring motion marks real state changes and stops when the user asks it to. AI stays ambient, labeled and easy to correct. Everly already has the right ingredients: a zero-scroll Home, voice capture that turns one sentence into several tasks, a companion orb, and a dark Manifest deck. What holds it back is structure more than looks. In the working tree I reviewed, Home, Ideas and Manifest are sibling `Stack` routes, and each draws its own copy of the bottom dock. Tapping a dock item therefore *pushes* a new screen instead of switching tabs. The "+" button exists only on Home, where it competes with the floating mic. The "3D Glass" theme produces no real blur on iOS or Android, because its only blur is a web-only CSS `backdropFilter`. The most valuable work is: one real tab navigator with three labelled destinations, one primary capture action, a glass fallback chain that respects Reduce Transparency, and accessibility basics (labels, contrast, Reduce Motion). Everything needed ships with Expo SDK 58 / React Native 0.88. One point is urgent. SDK 58's default EAS iOS image is Xcode 27.1, and Apple ignores the Liquid Glass opt-out in apps built with the iOS 27 SDK, so Everly's native headers and sheets turn into Liquid Glass on the next build whether or not anyone designed for it ([Expo upgrade guide](https://expo.dev/blog/how-to-upgrade-to-sdk-58); [Apple Dev Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/832543)).

## Both platforms float the chrome and keep content solid, and iOS 27 dialed glass back

Apple's Liquid Glass (WWDC, June 2025) treats translucency as a **separate functional layer for navigation and controls that floats above content**. The Human Interface Guidelines say plainly: "**Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer**" and "use Liquid Glass effects sparingly." Of its two variants, *Regular* blurs and adjusts luminosity for text-heavy chrome. *Clear* is reserved for rich media such as photos, with a **35% dimming layer** over bright imagery ([Apple HIG: Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)). The rollout caused real legibility problems. NN/g found that translucent controls over busy content were "nearly illegible" and that controls which morph, wiggle and collapse cost predictability. Its verdict was that Apple was "prioritizing spectacle over usability" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Apple then retreated in steps. iOS 26.1 added a Clear/Tinted toggle ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/04/ios-26-1-lets-you-turn-down-liquid-glass-transparency)). iOS 27 (June 2026) replaced the toggle with a continuous opacity slider and **lowered default transparency** ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/06/08/apple-updates-liquid-glass-ui-in-ios-27-with-new-slider-to-customize-how-it-looks/)). TapSmart reports that "toolbars are making a comeback" so floating buttons stop feeling disconnected ([TapSmart](https://www.tapsmart.com/news/wwdc26-recap/)). The direction for 2026 is legible glass on a few controls, not maximal translucency.

Google's Material 3 Expressive (May 2025, stable on Pixel with Android 16 QPR1) gets its expressiveness from **shape, color and spring motion rather than glass**. It replaces duration/easing curves with a physics-based motion system and adds 35 new shapes with morphing ([Google Blog](https://blog.google/products/android/material-3-expressive-android-wearos-launch/); [Supercharge Design](https://supercharge.design/blog/material-3-expressive)). It also adds a FAB menu for 2–6 actions and **deprecates the bottom app bar**, the component that used to hold a cradled center FAB, in favor of docked and floating toolbars ([MDC BottomAppBar.md](https://github.com/material-components/material-components-android/blob/master/docs/components/BottomAppBar.md); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/guides/material-design/)). Google reports 46 studies with 18,000+ participants in which users found key elements "up to four times faster". That is a vendor claim from a post Google briefly published and then pulled ([Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/google-leaks-the-reason-and-process-behind-their-new-material-3-expressive-design-language)). There is no first-party M3 Expressive kit for React Native, so Everly can only adopt its ideas: springs, pill shapes, emphasized type and a FAB menu.

Edge-to-edge is no longer optional on either platform. Android 16 removes the opt-out entirely. Expo has said since SDK 54 that edge-to-edge is "enabled in all Android apps, and cannot be disabled" ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16); [Expo SDK 54 changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-54)). Your recent inset fix on headers and the dock was the right baseline, but three follow-ups remain:

- **Scroll padding.** Scroll content still needs bottom padding equal to dock height plus `insets.bottom`. Ideas currently hardcodes `paddingBottom: 100`.
- **Predictive back.** Android 16 turns on predictive back by default ([Android 16 behavior changes](https://developer.android.com/about/versions/16/behavior-changes-16)). With today's push-based dock, a back gesture walks through every screen the user has visited instead of leaving the app.
- **iOS headers.** SDK 58 makes iOS 26+ native headers **transparent glass with content extending beneath them** by default ([Expo upgrade guide](https://expo.dev/blog/how-to-upgrade-to-sdk-58)). Check that the `add-reminder` modal, the one route with a native header, still clears its form.

The global `<StatusBar style="light" />` deserves a check too. Light icons fit the teal and dark headers, but they will likely vanish over the white header of a full-screen Android `add-reminder` modal.

## Everly's dock is a stack wearing a tab bar

Both platform guidelines agree on bottom navigation: **3–5 labelled destinations used only for navigation**. Apple writes "Use a tab bar to support navigation, not to provide actions… use a toolbar instead" and warns against screen-specific actions in the tab-bar accessory ([Apple HIG: Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars); [WWDC25 session 356](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/356/)). Android's navigation bar covers three to five equal destinations, and actions belong in a FAB or FAB menu ([Android Compose navigation bar](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/components/navigation-bar)). Everly's three destinations, Home · Ideas · Manifest, fit this range. The center "+" is the part that conflicts with the guidelines.

The case for keeping the "+" deserves a fair hearing. Rosebud, a well-regarded AI journal, ships five tabs with a center "+" for a new entry ([Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/wellness/rosebud-therapy-app-review-features-price)), and users understand the convention. Everly's problem is specific, though. Home already has a floating mic that creates tasks, so the "+" is a **second create button** on the same screen. It also **disappears on Ideas and Manifest**, where those screens' docks have three items. NN/g's criticism of controls that change location ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)) applies directly: a dock whose shape changes from screen to screen is harder to learn. The recommendation is to **make voice the single primary create action** and to demote typed entry to a secondary path, not delete it. Concretely: a floating mic button sits above the bar, and long-pressing it opens a small M3-style FAB menu with *Type a reminder*, *Capture idea* and *Record Voice Echo* (the menu pattern suits 2–6 actions). Tapping still starts voice capture on Home and Ideas. The dock shrinks to three evenly spaced, always-labelled tabs. The Apple HIG's warning against voice-only paths also argues for keeping "Type a reminder" one long-press away and inside the capture sheet itself ([Apple HIG Generative AI](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)).

The implementation choice in SDK 58 is between two tab architectures, and the trade-off is clear. **Native tabs** graduated to stable `expo-router/native-tabs` in SDK 58. They render a real UITabBar with Liquid Glass on iOS 26+ and a Material bar on Android, and they automatically follow the user's Clear/Tinted slider, Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast settings. SDK 58 also adds a `prominent` tab (Xcode 27), `minimizeBehavior="onScrollDown"` and `NativeTabs.BottomAccessory` ([Native tabs docs](https://docs.expo.dev/router/advanced/native-tabs/); [SDK 58 beta changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58-beta)). However, native tabs **cannot draw a raised center FAB**, "the tab bar height cannot be measured" (so a floating mic overlay cannot position itself reliably against the bar), and every tab screen renders eagerly. That last point matters for Manifest's photos and audio, which should defer work until focused. **JS tabs** now share the same `standard-navigation` integration as native tabs in SDK 58 ([Expo upgrade guide](https://expo.dev/blog/how-to-upgrade-to-sdk-58)) and keep full visual control. The cost is that the glass is fake and will not follow the user's iOS glass settings.

The decision is to ship in two steps. First, move the three screens into a `(tabs)` group rendered through expo-router's JS `Tabs`, or headless `expo-router/ui` tabs, with **one** shared `BottomDock`. Keep `add-reminder` as a modal on the root stack, and put the mic in a single overlay. This fixes push-stacking, predictive back and dock drift in one change, and it runs in Expo Go. Verify the custom-tab-bar API against SDK 58 docs first, because the navigation core was reworked and `withLayoutContext` is no longer the recommended path. Second, in a development build, prototype `NativeTabs` with the capture control as a `BottomAccessory`. Capture is app-wide and persistent, which is the case the HIG reserves the accessory for, and the accessory removes the bar-height measurement problem. Adopt it if it reads well. Skip hide-on-scroll entirely. Home is zero-scroll by design, and NN/g counts collapsing bars against learnability.

## Glass belongs on Everly's chrome, warmth on its content

Neumorphism has largely left production UI because its same-color edges fail WCAG's 3:1 non-text contrast. It survives only on a single hero control ([setproduct](https://www.setproduct.com/blog/neumorphism-design-guide)). Glass is now a platform default and is safe when confined to floating chrome over backgrounds you control. NN/g's glassmorphism guidance calls for more blur, a hairline stroke to define edges, contrast testing against *every* color that can appear behind the element, and a reduce-transparency path ([NN/g glassmorphism](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/glassmorphism/)). Everly's "3D Glass" theme currently does the opposite on native. Its cards are `rgba(255,255,255,0.12)` with `muted` text at 72% opacity, there is no blur at all outside web, and the backdrop is four saturated radial "orbs". Design-trend catalogs now treat aurora and mesh gradients as "genre shorthand for 'AI product'" ([Enlighten design-styles](https://design-styles.enlighten-media.net/pages/aurora/)). These cards fail the brief twice: they look generic, and their contrast shifts with whatever orb sits behind them.

The fix is a tiered material, sketched below:

1. **iOS 26+ with Liquid Glass available:** `GlassView`, `regular` style, with `colorScheme` forced from Everly's own theme toggle. Guard it with `isGlassEffectAPIAvailable() && isLiquidGlassAvailable() && !reduceTransparency`.
2. **Other iOS:** `BlurView` with a system material tint.
3. **Android:** a near-opaque tinted surface by default, with `BlurView` (`blurMethod="dimezisBlurViewSdk31Plus"`) only where content and dock share a `BlurTargetView`.
4. **Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast on:** solid surfaces.

`expo-glass-effect` falls back to a plain `View` everywhere except iOS 26+, and the docs warn that glass disappears below about 1.0 opacity, so animate `glassEffectStyle` toward `'none'` instead of fading it ([Expo GlassEffect docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/glass-effect/)). Android blur has an architectural catch. A dock rendered once in a layout cannot easily target each screen's content ([Expo BlurView docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/blur-view/)). That is another reason a solid, Material-style docked bar is the right Android answer: M3's navigation bar is docked and full-width anyway. `GlassContainer` with `spacing` can merge the dock pill and mic button into one morphing glass shape on iOS. Keep reminder cards, idea cards and affirmation text on opaque or near-opaque surfaces in every theme. The one sanctioned use of *clear* glass in Everly is the Manifest pillar cards, where text sits over photography. There, add the 35% dim layer.

Home's category quadrants are a bento grid in all but name, and bento on mobile works only when **cards are ranked by importance and sized on purpose**. A uniform stack becomes an endless scroll ([saasframe](https://www.saasframe.io/blog/designing-bento-grids-that-actually-work-a-2026-practical-guide); [Inkbot Design](https://inkbotdesign.com/bento-grid-design)). The zero-scroll first viewport should answer three questions:

- **What's my focus?** The companion and Sync score.
- **What's next?** A single "Next up" hero row drawn from whichever category is due soonest, above the quadrants.
- **How do I capture?** The mic.

Today's quadrants are color-coded by a small dot and a colored top border. Add a per-category icon, because color alone fails both Apple's accessibility guidance and color-blind users ([Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)). These mixed sizes also map directly onto small, medium and large widgets later.

Typography is the cheapest way to give Everly character. SDK 58 adds **true variable-font support in `expo-font`**, and RN 0.88 adds `fontVariationSettings` on `Text` ([SDK 58 beta changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-58-beta)). One file can replace a set of static weights. Everly currently loads only SpaceMono and otherwise uses the system font. Design forecasters describe a 2026 swing toward warm, soft serifs alongside neo-grotesque sans ([Envato](https://elements.envato.com/learn/font-trends); [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/trending-fonts/)). Wellness brands lean on humanist type with "playful to clinical" range, as in Headspace's custom Aperçu ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/italic-studio-headspace-graphic-design-project-250424)). For Everly, that suggests a variable serif for affirmations and Manifest headings and a humanist or rounded variable sans for UI. The pairing is my inference: no source documents serif adoption in wellness apps specifically. Color direction is similar. Pantone's 2026 Color of the Year is **Cloud Dancer**, a soft off-white chosen for calm ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/?p=2275626)). Rosebud runs white plus one saturated accent ([Rosebud press kit](https://help.rosebud.app/about-us/press-kit)). Everly's teal is already a strong single accent. Warm the Light theme's pure white toward an off-white, keep Dark on near-black, and on Android 12+ consider an opt-in "System" accent through Expo Router's `Color.android.dynamic.*` ([Expo Router Color](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/router/color/)).

Motion should feel physical and purposeful. Springs paired with haptics are the shared standard. Apple's guidance is to "make motion optional", and NN/g's critique of iOS 26 targets animation "without user benefit" ([Hacking with Swift](https://www.hackingwithswift.com/quick-start/swiftui/how-to-detect-the-reduce-motion-accessibility-setting); [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/liquid-glass/)). Everly's companion orb runs a permanent 60-BPM `Animated.loop` even when idle, and the Ideas mic FAB pulses the same way. Both use the legacy `Animated` API, although Reanimated 4.7.1 with worklets 0.13.0, a supported pairing for RN 0.88, is already installed ([Reanimated compatibility](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/guides/compatibility)). Move these loops to Reanimated. Make the idle state still or barely breathing, and save energetic motion for listening, task completion and streak moments. Use a short haptic vocabulary: selection ticks on chips and tabs, a light impact on mic start, a success notification on "Add all". Today haptics appear only in `HypeInterstitial` and `RealityLock`. Shared-element transitions remain experimental and do not work across tabs ([Reanimated SET docs](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/shared-element-transitions/overview)), so don't build the experience on them.

Among wellness apps, the companion-led camp has the strongest 2025 signals. Finch's widgets and forgiving pet loop are credited with roughly 54%/37% D1/D7 retention, a third-party estimate that shows correlation, not causation ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/x0hd2ssr80y5n7gv0w967pg7hwd7tl)). Focus Friend's bean avatar won Google Play's Best App of 2025 ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/google-play/best-apps-games-2025/)). The minimal-editorial camp (Stoic, Rosebud) owns calm reading surfaces. Everly already sits between the two: a companion on Home and an editorial, photo-led Manifest. That split is the right one. Keep the companion off the Manifest reading surface except at milestones.

## The companion should be ambient, honest and quick to correct

Flagship assistants have moved from full-screen orbs to lighter, in-context presences. ChatGPT folded voice into the chat thread with a live transcript in November 2025 ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/chatgpt-voice-mode-in-chat-3619633)). Gemini Live is moving to a floating overlay with a central waveform, according to beta teardowns ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/04/07/gemini-live-redesign-android/)). Siri swapped its orb for an edge glow that **reacts to voice amplitude** ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2024/11/03/new-apple-intelligence-siri-looks-different-works-the-same/)). The lesson for Everly's orb is that animation should mean something. Its states (idle, listening, speaking, reacting) should be driven by mic amplitude and real app events, not by a metronome. Its mood should follow real progress, the way Finch's pet does, and missed days should never be punished with guilt. Finch's pet stays "happy to see" returning users ([IU School of Medicine](https://geneticslab.medicine.iu.edu/blogs/md-student-news/wellness-corner-a-digital-pet-is-your-self-care-best-friend)). The **Sync score** needs one tap to an explanation, per Microsoft's HAX guidelines 2 and 11 ("make clear how well it can do" and "why the system did what it did") ([HAX Toolkit](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)).

Apple's Generative AI HIG (updated June 2026) gives the trust checklist. Everly should clearly identify where AI is used and never imply a human author. It should put Edit/Undo/Retry next to AI output, confirm significant actions, and replace "Processing…" with specific progress text. It should also **ask people for personal attributes rather than inferring them**, and disclose server use and training ([Apple HIG Generative AI](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)). That last point lands squarely on a feature Everly already has. `moodFromTranscript` infers a stress state from speech (a `rapid_speech` signal) and has the companion respond to it. Make this visible and switchable: say "Everly noticed you sounded rushed" rather than silently adapting, and provide a setting to turn mood sensing off. Proactive nudges such as `WarmInboundBubble` should be dismissible in one tap, carry a global quiet-hours or frequency control (HAX 3, 8 and 17), and never auto-dismiss on a timer.

For voice capture, the closest reference is **Todoist Ramble** (beta, January 2026). Tasks appear as live preview cards while the user speaks. "Actually, I meant…" and "Scratch that" correct by voice. Users can change the project or label before committing, and an explicit "that's all" or checkmark ends the session ([Todoist Help](https://www.todoist.com/help/articles/dictate-and-add-tasks-with-ramble-P1Raq7vVF)). Doist's main build lesson is that "**easy correction beats perfect first-time accuracy**". The model is tuned to capture literally rather than act ([Product Talk](https://www.producttalk.org/building-todoist-ramble-how-doist-turned-voice-braindumps-into-real-time-task-capture/)). Everly's `VoiceCapture` already parses, previews and commits with "Add N". Its preview, however, is a plain text list. Upgrade each item to a card with tappable chips:

- category, with icon and label
- date and time
- a discard ✕

Add a grouped summary ("3 tasks · 1 Personal · 2 Professional") and an amplitude-reactive listening visual next to a live transcript. After commit, show an Undo snackbar that stays until dismissed. Never auto-run side effects, such as messaging someone about a Social task. The Ideas vault should follow AudioPen and Voicenotes: keep the raw transcript and show the AI-cleaned version as an editable layer on top ([Voicenotes](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/voicenotes-ai-notes-meetings/id6483293628?l=vi)). Present AI clustering as labelled, editable suggestions ("Everly grouped these"), and resurface related ideas in context, as Mem does ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/10/mem-2-0-relaunches-with-smarter-note-taking-tools-offline-support-and-a-new-agentic-chat)). Never silently move or delete ideas.

## Accessibility is now a legal floor, and Everly's dock misses it

The European Accessibility Act has been enforceable since **28 June 2025**. It applies to non-EU businesses serving EU consumers (micro-enterprises are exempt), and it works through EN 301 549, which references WCAG 2.1 AA ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/interface/2025/accessibility/the-european-accessibility-act-and-its-implementation); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2025/07/european-accessibility-act-digital-products)). The practical targets:

- **Target size:** at least 24×24 px under WCAG 2.2 ([W3C 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), and Apple's 44×44 pt default (28 pt minimum).
- **Contrast:** 4.5:1 for text up to 17 pt, checked in both light and dark.
- **Text scaling:** text must scale to 200%.
- **Color:** never color alone.
- **Reduce Motion:** cut repetitive animation when it is on.
- **Audio:** captions or transcripts for audio-only content, and no autoplay without controls.

([Apple HIG Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility))

Everly's dock fails several of these today:

- **Icons:** the tab icons are text glyphs and emoji (⌂, 💡, ✦), with no `accessibilityRole="tab"`, no selected state and no labels. Across all screens and components there are only eight `accessibilityLabel`s.
- **Unused symbols package:** `expo-symbols` is installed but unused. Swapping in `SymbolView` with per-platform SF Symbols and Material Symbols names fixes the icon semantics and the off-platform look at once ([expo-symbols docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/symbols/)).
- **Motion:** the orb's idle pulse has no Reduce Motion path. Pair Reanimated's synchronous `useReducedMotion()`, which reflects only the setting at launch, with an `AccessibilityInfo` `reduceMotionChanged` listener to catch changes mid-session ([Reanimated useReducedMotion](https://docs.swmansion.com/react-native-reanimated/docs/device/useReducedMotion)).
- **Glass theme contrast:** the 3D Glass theme needs contrast testing against its real, shifting backdrop, not a mockup.
- **Mic button:** it needs a state announcement ("listening" or "not listening") and the typed alternative already in the sheet. Apple's Vocal Shortcuts and Google's Project Relate exist because standard recognition fails people with atypical speech ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-hk/guide/iphone/iph7f242ea2c); [AbilityNet](https://abilitynet.org.uk/news-blogs/how-project-relate-can-help-those-atypical-speech-patterns)).
- **Manifest deck:** frequency tones and Voice Echo playback need visible controls, transcripts for any spoken affirmations, and an on-screen button for every swipe gesture.

## A prioritized roadmap, sized for SDK 58

A few SDK 58 facts set the constraints:

- **Expo Go:** the store versions of Expo Go lag behind SDK 58. Until they update, iOS devices use `eas go`, and Expo recommends development builds for new projects ([Expo upgrade guide](https://expo.dev/blog/how-to-upgrade-to-sdk-58)).
- **Builds:** the `sdk-58` and `latest` EAS iOS images now use Xcode 27.1, so production builds get forced Liquid Glass.
- **Dependencies:** `expo-symbols` and `@expo/ui` are optional peer dependencies of expo-router and must be installed explicitly. `expo-symbols` already is.
- **Already in Expo Go:** `expo-glass-effect`, `expo-blur`, `expo-symbols` and `@expo/ui` are all marked "Included in Expo Go". Real glass appears only on an iOS 26+ device or simulator, and true iOS 27-SDK behavior needs a development build on the Xcode 27.1 image ([GlassEffect docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/glass-effect/); [BlurView docs](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/sdk/blur-view/)).

Effort below is my estimate: S is under a day, M is 1–3 days, L is a week or more.

## Conclusion

The 2026 design story is a correction. Apple spent a year walking Liquid Glass back toward legibility, and Google built expressiveness from shape and motion instead of translucency. Both moves reward apps that treat effects as a thin chrome layer over sturdy, readable content. For Everly, the uncomfortable takeaway is that the visual trend work matters less than the plumbing it would sit on. A push-based "tab" bar, a theme system that doesn't reach the screens, and glass that only exists on web would undercut any amount of polish. Fixing the structure first also makes later polish cheap: one dock to glass up, one capture button to animate, and one theme token set to tune.

The more distinctive opportunity is in Everly's AI. Voice capture that splits one sentence into several tasks, and a companion that senses mood, are exactly the features the 2025–2026 guidance is most cautious about. The design wins there come from correction and disclosure: editable chips, visible reasoning behind the Sync score, and an off switch for mood sensing. Those, more than any surface effect, will decide whether Everly reads as a calm companion or as a gimmick. Two open questions are worth testing with real users: whether a mic-first capture model leaves typed entry discoverable enough, and whether native tabs' platform fidelity outweighs the custom dock's brand character.
